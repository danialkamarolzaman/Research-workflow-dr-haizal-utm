# Detailed seven-step research workflow

Read this procedure when applying the researcher-workflow skill. Apply all stages for a full review; for a focused request, use the requested stages and necessary prerequisites. The Introduction is the default target in a full workflow; replace it with the section the user selects. Select methodological checks for the discipline and study type; mark irrelevant checks as not applicable. Suggested counts are priorities, not quotas: do not invent findings to fill them.

## STEP 1 — INTERROGATE

Challenge the research before editing its language.

### ROLE
Act as a skeptical and experienced researcher.

### TASK
Using M1 and available supporting evidence, identify:
1. The core research problem and why it matters.
2. What the supplied evidence establishes, and what is only asserted.
3. Explicit and implicit assumptions.
4. Weaknesses, ambiguities, internal inconsistencies, and unresolved issues.
5. The possible research gap and contribution.
6. Evidence required to defend each important claim.
7. Claims that exceed the demonstrated results.
8. Alternative explanations that remain plausible, clearly labelled as hypotheses.

Do not rewrite the manuscript at this stage. Distinguish findings within the study from broader practical, theoretical, or societal implications that require additional evidence.

### OUTPUT
Observation | Source and location | Evidence/inference status | Concern | Evidence needed | Recommended researcher decision

Use ACCEPT, MODIFY, REJECT, or VERIFY as recommendations, with a short reason. ACCEPT means suitable to carry forward within the stated evidence boundary; it does not certify research validity.

### SELF-CRITIQUE
Challenge your own first assessment. Identify its strongest unsupported assumption, any contrary evidence, and what information could change the conclusion. Refine the assessment once.

### CARRY FORWARD
Create a concise Step 1 brief containing:
- Selected research problem.
- Strongest supplied evidence.
- Key uncertainty or candidate gap.
- Claims requiring verification.
- Evidence needed for the next stages.

## STEP 2 — SYNTHESIZE

Connect evidence across papers rather than summarizing papers one by one.

### ROLE
Act as a critical researcher conducting literature synthesis.

### CONTEXT
Use the Step 1 brief and the supplied papers. Prefer 3–5 directly relevant papers if available; include additional supplied papers when they materially affect the comparison. Explain exclusions and limitations.

### TASK
Identify common approaches, major differences, trade-offs, contradictions, limitations, unresolved issues, possible gaps, and the closest supplied prior work.

Before comparing findings, assess compatibility of research questions, populations or cases, contexts, study designs, source types, definitions, outcomes, analytic methods, timeframes, and evidence quality. Distinguish observed, self-reported, experimental, simulated, derived, and interpretive findings as applicable. Use “not reported,” “not comparable,” or “not checked” rather than filling gaps.

For comparisons across studies:
- Compare like constructs, outcomes, or interpretive questions; explain differing definitions and conditions.
- Preserve denominators, units, scope, uncertainty, and analytic assumptions where applicable.
- Do not rank studies from isolated best-case findings or treat incompatible evidence as directly comparable.
- Preserve contextual and methodological differences in qualitative, historical, or theoretical work rather than forcing a numeric ranking.

Do not generalize from a few papers to “most designs” or “all existing work.” Treat review-paper summaries as secondary evidence until their cited primary studies are available.

### OUTPUT
Pattern | Supporting source(s) and location(s) | Difference/trade-off | Limitation | Unresolved issue | Implication for this study

Also provide a bounded comparison of the closest supplied prior work:
Source | Question/context | Study design or source approach | Key findings and definitions | Evidence/validation basis | Comparability limits

### EVIDENCE CHAIN
Established knowledge → Supported limitation → Unresolved issue → Why it matters → What this study could address.

### SELF-CRITIQUE AND CARRY FORWARD
Identify contradictory evidence and the strongest alternative interpretation. Carry forward the best-supported pattern, limitation, closest comparison candidates, and their evidence locations. If no relevant papers are accessible, give the comparison framework and mark the literature-supported gap unassessed.

## STEP 3 — IDENTIFY THE REAL GAP

Test novelty rather than assume it.

### ROLE
Act as a skeptical journal researcher and reviewer.

### CONTEXT
Use the Step 1 research challenge and Step 2 synthesis.

### TASK
Propose a bounded gap and contribution, then test them for specificity, evidence support, importance, feasibility, and defensibility.

Distinguish:
- A demonstrated limitation in prior work.
- A question still unresolved by the supplied evidence.
- A missing comparison in this manuscript.
- A merely unverified assertion of novelty.

Identify the closest supplied prior work, the claimed genuine difference, supporting evidence, the strongest reviewer objection, and evidence still required.

Classify the proposed contribution, with reasons:
A. Incremental improvement.
B. Design, framework, or research-resource contribution.
C. Methodological contribution.
D. Conceptual, theoretical, or interpretive insight.
E. Potentially substantial contribution.

Categories may overlap. “Potentially substantial” remains provisional until justified. A different setting, dataset, sample, label, or better reported metric alone does not establish a meaningful contribution. Explain whether the work adds replication evidence, a boundary condition, an improved method, a new interpretation, a theoretical result, or another defensible advance.

### OUTPUT
Candidate gap | Closest prior work and location | Genuine difference | Evidence supporting gap | Reviewer objection | Evidence still needed | Contribution classification

### GAP JUDGEMENT
### YES / PARTLY / NO / NOT ASSESSABLE FROM SUPPLIED EVIDENCE.
Explain the judgement and its scope. Do not claim an exhaustive novelty search. Judge novelty relative to the stated contribution: a well-justified replication or evidence synthesis need not claim a new mechanism or theory.

### CARRY FORWARD
Provide:
1. A defensible gap statement, or an explicitly provisional design question.
2. A bounded contribution statement.
3. The strongest verified comparison available.
4. Conditions that must be satisfied before stronger claims can be used.

## STEP 4 — STRUCTURE THE RESEARCH STORY

Build the research argument before drafting prose.

### ROLE
Act as a critical research supervisor.

### CONTEXT
Use Steps 1–3 and the supplied objective, approach, results, and proposed contribution. Label inferred objectives or interpretations as provisional.

### TASK
Build:
### PROBLEM → GAP → OBJECTIVE → APPROACH → EVIDENCE → CONTRIBUTION

For each element, provide one precise sentence and its supporting source or verification status.

Check whether:
1. The objective addresses the identified gap.
2. The approach can answer the objective.
3. The reported results actually answer it.
4. The evidence supports the claimed contribution.
5. The conclusions go beyond the tested conditions.
6. Any link depends on a missing comparison, causal explanation, or measurement.

### WRITING BRIEF
Prepare a brief for Section 1.0 Introduction that states:
- Purpose and central message.
- Research problem and relevant established knowledge.
- Supported limitations in prior work.
- Defensible gap or provisional design question.
- Objective and approach.
- Results that can safely be mentioned, with observed, modeled, derived, or interpretive status.
- Bounded contribution.
- Claims the section must not make.
- Intended reader takeaway.
- Proposed purpose and logical sequence of its paragraphs.

### OUTPUT
A. Research story with evidence links.
B. Writing brief.
C. Missing evidence and unresolved links.
D. Recommended researcher decisions.

### CARRY FORWARD
Consolidate a Step 1–4 research brief. This is the explicit input to Step 5. If a gap remains unproven, frame the study as evaluating a specified research question rather than asserting established novelty.

## STEP 5 — WRITE

Use the research brief to diagnose, organise, and rewrite the target section.

### ROLE
Act as an experienced academic editor.

### CONTEXT
Use the Step 1–4 research brief and the original Introduction.

### FOUR-MOVE WORKFLOW
1. PURPOSE: State what each paragraph must communicate and how it supports the research story.
2. DIAGNOSE: Identify unclear, repetitive, unsupported, overstated, or disconnected passages. Cite the original wording or paragraph location.
3. ORGANISE: Propose the clearest paragraph sequence and explain how it supports the key message.
4. REWRITE: Produce the complete revised Introduction after presenting the diagnosis and organisation.

### CONSTRAINTS
Preserve supported scholarly meaning, discipline-specific detail, units, numerical values, and the distinction between observed, modeled, derived, and interpretive findings. Do not invent citations, results, mechanisms, equations, comparisons, or substantive claims.

Do not preserve an unsupported claim as established fact merely to retain the original wording. Soften, qualify, omit, or visibly flag it, and document the change. Do not silently resolve conflicting numbers.

Keep valid citation identifiers attached only to claims they support. When support has not been checked, use a visible [CITATION TO VERIFY] or [AUTHOR VERIFICATION NEEDED] marker. Do not silently renumber references or manufacture bibliographic entries.

If a complete rewrite is not supportable, provide a provisional draft with specific placeholders. Avoid implying causation, superiority, universal applicability, or practical readiness unless the supplied evidence supports it. Preserve uncertainty, alternative interpretations, and appropriate scope.

### OUTPUT
A. Paragraph purpose and diagnosis.
B. Recommended organisation.
C. Complete revised Introduction.
D. Meaning/evidence audit:
Original claim/location | Revised claim | Meaning preserved or intentionally qualified? | Supporting evidence | New/unsupported claim? | Author action
E. Unresolved author-verification items.

### FOLLOW-UP CHECK
Compare the revised section against both the original and the Step 1–4 brief:
- Has the key message remained consistent?
- Is each substantive claim supported?
- Have any new mechanisms or interpretations appeared?
- Does each paragraph support the gap, objective, or contribution where appropriate?
- What must the author personally change or verify?

Correct unintended changes and present the resulting draft.

## STEP 6 — ATTACK THE PAPER

Expose substantive weaknesses and turn them into actions.

### ROLE
Act as a skeptical reviewer for a high-quality scholarly journal.

### CONTEXT
Review the complete manuscript and Step 1–4 brief. Also evaluate the revised Introduction, clearly distinguishing draft improvements from unresolved problems elsewhere.

### REVIEWER LENSES
1. NOVELTY AND CONTRIBUTION
Is the contribution differentiated from the closest supplied work? Is the gap evidence-supported? Is the claimed insight more than a performance change?

2. METHODOLOGY AND TECHNICAL RIGOR
Are the research design, assumptions, source or participant selection, collection procedures, analytic choices, and relevant methods sufficiently clear to assess the argument? Apply study-appropriate checks: quantitative work may require bias, confounding, missingness, uncertainty, and model assumptions; qualitative work may require sampling rationale, analytic traceability, reflexivity, and contextual depth; theoretical work may require definitions, premises, derivations, and counterexamples; historical or humanities work may require source provenance, contextual interpretation, and engagement with alternative readings; evidence syntheses may require search, selection, appraisal, and synthesis transparency. Assess mixed-method integration when relevant. Check ethics, consent, registration, or reporting requirements only where applicable, and distinguish absent reporting from confirmed noncompliance.

3. VALIDATION AND EVIDENCE
Do findings support the claims? Are direct observations, participant accounts, calculations, model outputs, and interpretations distinguished? Assess the reliability, credibility, uncertainty, robustness, reproducibility, or transferability appropriate to the study type and material to the conclusion. Do not impose one universal validation standard across disciplines.

4. STATE OF THE ART AND SIGNIFICANCE
Are comparisons fair and relevant? Are central constructs defined consistently? Does the claimed scholarly or practical significance follow from the evidence? Are limitations and the boundaries of generalization or interpretation acknowledged?

### CONSTRAINTS
Base criticisms on supplied material and identified absences. “Not reported” does not mean “not performed.” Recommend new analyses or experiments only as proposed actions with a reason; never imply they have already been completed.

### OUTPUT
Reviewer lens | Concern and source location | Severity: minor/major/critical | Evidence needed | Proposed author action

Use critical for issues that undermine a central conclusion; major for substantial evidence or reporting gaps; minor for localized issues that do not change the research conclusion.

### CARRY FORWARD
Rank the five most consequential revisions. For each, state whether the remedy requires clearer reporting, source verification, reanalysis, additional evidence collection or validation, or narrower claims. Identify which earlier workflow step must be revisited.

## STEP 7 — VERIFY THE PAPER

Audit claims against evidence without overstating what verification establishes.

### ROLE
Act as a research-integrity and evidence-verification assistant.

### TASK
Audit important research claims, numerical values, citations, equations, mechanisms, novelty, dimensions, comparisons, interpretations, and conclusions. Cross-check abstract, Introduction, methods, results, tables, figures, and conclusion for internal consistency.

Select verification checks appropriate to the study:
- Source identity, provenance, citation accuracy, and whether cited passages support the associated claim.
- Consistency of questions, constructs, definitions, methods, findings, and conclusions.
- For quantitative work: sample sizes, denominators, units, descriptive summaries, effect sizes, uncertainty, statistical assumptions, missing data, and multiple comparisons where relevant.
- For qualitative work: consistency between source material, coding or interpretive procedures, themes, illustrative quotations, contrary cases, and contextual claims, where the materials permit checking.
- For computational work: data provenance, preprocessing, leakage, evaluation design, baselines, parameters, code or environment availability, and uncertainty where relevant.
- For theoretical or mathematical work: definitions, premises, logical steps, derivations, boundary conditions, and counterexamples within the available expertise and evidence.
- For historical or humanities work: source context, translation or quotation fidelity, chronology, interpretive warrants, and treatment of competing readings.
- For evidence synthesis: search and selection records, duplicate handling, source appraisal, extraction accuracy, and synthesis assumptions where applicable.
- Whether claims of causation, novelty, generalizability, transferability, or practical significance match the actual evidence and study design.

Recalculate only when required inputs and definitions are available; show the formula and inputs. Do not guess missing terms in equations. If extraction loses mathematical notation or figure detail, report the access limitation rather than declaring the original document defective.

### STATUS DEFINITIONS
VERIFIED WITHIN PROVIDED EVIDENCE: a stated check supports the claim; specify exactly what was checked.
PARTLY VERIFIED: some aspects are supported but material conditions remain unchecked.
UNVERIFIED: evidence is absent, inaccessible, or insufficient.
CONTRADICTED: identified supplied evidence conflicts with the claim.

A consistent manuscript statement is only “reported and internally consistent,” not independently validated. Inspecting a plot, quotation, or summary does not establish the integrity of its underlying data or sources.

### OUTPUT
Claim and location | Evidence available | Verification status and scope | Risk | What must be checked | Author action

Include unresolved conflicts and results of any supported calculations. Avoid claiming exhaustive validation when underlying data, full sources, or analytic records are unavailable.

## FINAL CONSOLIDATION AND RESEARCH ACTION PLAN

Complete the workflow and check your own output.

### INTEGRATION
Carry material findings from Steps 6–7 back into the gap, research story, and rewritten Introduction. Identify which sections changed and why. Present one final consolidated Introduction; keep unresolved verification markers visible. Do not hide uncertainty to make the writing sound stronger.

### SUBMISSION PACKAGING CHECK
Assess consistency among the title, abstract, Introduction, contribution, and conclusion. Suggest bounded corrections where needed. Do not rewrite the whole manuscript unless requested. If journal-specific AI or submission rules are needed but not supplied, mark them as unchecked rather than inventing policy.

### PRACTICAL USE BOUNDARIES
Distinguish work AI can assist with from decisions requiring author judgement: final gap selection, novelty, interpretation, research claims, citation validity, and submission approval. Do not expose confidential content to additional services or sources without authorization.

### FINAL OUTPUTS
1. The three most consequential research risks.
2. The three most important evidence gaps.
3. The three highest-priority manuscript revisions.
4. Claims to soften, qualify, remove, or verify, with locations.
5. Citations requiring checking and the exact claim each should support.
6. A concise action plan:
Priority | Action | Evidence/resource needed | Completion criterion | Suggested timing
Include the next practical research action and a realistic next-24-hour action.
7. A bounded readiness judgement:
READY WITHIN THE STATED REVIEW SCOPE / REQUIRES MAJOR REVISION / NOT YET DEFENSIBLE / INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE TO ASSESS.
Explain the reason and remaining limits. This is not journal acceptance or independent certification of research validity.

### FINAL SELF-AUDIT
Confirm that you:
- Completed all seven steps and carried evidence forward.
- Challenged and refined the initial assessment.
- Supplied the full rewritten target section when supportable.
- Kept citations and source locations traceable.
- Distinguished reported results from verified claims.
- Did not invent sources, numerical values, mechanisms, or completed work.
- Kept recommendations separate from author-approved decisions.

End with:
Can I personally defend every important sentence in this manuscript using the relevant evidence, sources, analyses, and reasoning?
